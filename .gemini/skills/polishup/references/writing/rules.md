# Writing Rules

Apply to generated natural-language text, including explanations, UI copy, documents, code comments, and review comments. Use the [Writing index](index.md) when a decision needs deeper guidance.

## Required rules

- Never generate an em dash (U+2014). Preserve it only within source material that must remain verbatim, including quotations, code, data, or other immutable content. Elsewhere use commas, periods, parentheses, or restructure the sentence. Check new text before delivery without normalizing protected source text.
- Do not fabricate factual claims or evidence, including statistics, references, quotations, testimonials, or credentials. Use a verifiable basis and identify material verification gaps. A real citation must support the attached claim. Do not invent specificity, personal experience, opposing views, or consensus, or hide unsupported claims behind vague authority such as "experts say".

## Default writing style

- For informational and action-oriented work, give the answer or action early. Use familiar words and established terms when equally precise; avoid invented technical labels or awkward synonyms. Make actors, actions, conditions, and consequences explicit where needed. Prefer digits for quantities that readers need to scan or compare, preserving exact values and project conventions.
- Remove empty introductions, generic filler, repeated points, and padding. Avoid dramatic hooks, exaggerated stakes, stock metaphors, and inflated benefits. Replace hype and corporate abstractions with supported information. State uncertainty specifically instead of stacking vague hedges.
- Explain the actual relationship or useful detail instead of repeating a fact as an insight. Do not append unsupported benefits or significance, such as "ensuring better outcomes". Add supported explanation, qualify the inference, or omit it; do not invent detail to make the sentence specific.
- Use a measured, courteous tone for routine professional work. Avoid forced enthusiasm, familiarity, reassurance, and flattery. Do not agree with a factual claim just to please the reader or invent balance between unsupported positions. Match the requested genre and authorial character; preserve meaningful creative expression and necessary depth.
- Use headings, lists, bold, emoji, and punctuation only when they help meaning or navigation. In routine informational writing, avoid stock "not X, but Y" reframing, self-answered questions, forced lists, and dramatic pauses. State the point directly; retain devices needed for an actual distinction or requested style.
- Avoid sentence-by-sentence connectors, forced three-part slogans, repeated punchy fragments, and uniform paragraph templates. Preserve meaningful transitions, parallelism, and cadence; do not randomize rhythm or terminology.
- Omit unrequested chat introductions, process narration, placeholder citations, and repetitive summaries from the delivered artifact. Retain necessary explanations and requested drafting notes.

Preserve facts, qualifications, technical precision, and protected source text. Adapt defaults to language, genre, audience, and voice; the required rules retain their stated limits. Do not introduce mistakes, forced slang, or invented details to lower a detector score.

## Check the result

Check clarity, useful detail, support for implied claims, information and voice preservation, useful formatting, and U+2014 outside protected text. Repair the affected passage without rewriting satisfactory text. Keep necessary depth; expand when needed for understanding.

Evidence and examples: [Pew's 2026 language-pattern analysis](https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/08/20/how-much-of-the-internet-is-written-with-ai/) and [Paralect's practitioner examples](https://www.paralect.com/blog/signs-of-ai-slop-in-writing). Aggregate patterns and checklists do not establish individual authorship or universal language rules. The em dash prohibition is a project requirement; no frequency quotas or detector targets apply.
