# Art Direction

Use to establish or compare visual concepts, research inspiration, select imagery, adapt crops, or check representation and reuse. [Visual Design](visual-design.md) covers hierarchy and layout; [UX Research](ux-research.md) covers unresolved product needs.

## Define visual direction

Reuse purpose, primary tasks, audience, real content, identity, platform, and delivery constraints. Preserve an established direction except for changes within the requested scope; a mismatch can justify a repair or a proposal for wider work. Judge the specific surface: the same product's marketing page, work dashboard, documentation, and gallery can need different density, emphasis, and expression.

Translate vague adjectives into observable choices. "Professional" or "modern" does not specify a restrained palette, sparse layout, or enterprise style. Connect intended character to content, imagery, type, density, and interaction, using relevant audience, language, and cultural context. Do not assign a palette, typeface, or layout solely from an industry label.

Apply [PolishUp's default pattern restrictions](rules.md) when choosing a new direction. Do not select an excluded preset because a gallery features it or it seems appropriate without user direction. Preserve the requested or relevant existing identity; adapt its execution rather than reopening settled choices.

When direction is missing, choose a coherent relationship between content order, layout, type, color, density, assets, and interaction before repeating significant visual choices. Explain important choices in the existing deliverable when useful; no separate brief or token catalog is required. A small component repair needs no new identity, moodboard, or competitor study.

## Research visual references

Identify the unresolved question before browsing: explaining an unfamiliar workflow, showing product scale, communicating atmosphere, or adapting identity across formats. Search comparable tasks, audiences, platforms, and constraints. A generic search for "modern SaaS" rarely establishes those requirements.

| Source | Useful contribution | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live product or recorded flow | Content, patterns, interactions, and observable states | Production use does not prove effectiveness; recordings may omit paths or be outdated |
| Studio or brand case study | Stated intent, constraints, and coordinated execution | Selective presentation and promotional outcome claims |
| Gallery, award entry, or concept | Visual alternatives worth exploring | May omit real content, states, access, and delivery; awards do not establish task success |
| User study | Evidence within its tasks and methods | Not a universal visual formula |
| Project system | Local conventions and assets | Not automatically suitable for another product |

Choose a source by the question; these are starting points, not a required browsing list.

| Place to look | Use for | Check before adapting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/) | Shipped web/mobile screens and multi-step product flows | Capture date, platform, adjacent states, and access limits |
| [Lapa Ninja](https://www.lapa.ninja/) | Landing-page composition and product communication | Actual live page and content fit; archived versions may differ |
| [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/) | Expressive websites, art direction, and motion possibilities | Task suitability, readability, responsive behavior, and interaction cost |
| [Behance](https://www.behance.net/) | Brand systems, coordinated imagery, and presented case studies | Whether the work shipped and which outcomes are independently supported |
| [Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/) | Component treatments and visual alternatives | Whether a shot is a concept and whether states, content, and flows are represented |

Use actual comparable products for behavior and existing [design-system guidance](design-systems.md) for conventions. Galleries and awards do not establish usability or accessibility. No particular account, subscription, MCP, or tool is required; use accessible alternatives if a source is unavailable. Inspect beyond the thumbnail when possible, retaining the URL and useful creator/version context. Distinguish current behavior from old captures or unshipped concepts; a screenshot cannot prove unshown interaction.

For each reference that shapes a decision, note the useful principle, why it fits, and what to exclude. Adapt composition, typography, imagery, or interaction to the project. Do not copy another product's identity or combine incompatible styles.

Cross-check consequential disputed choices when another source could change the decision. Stop when more browsing is unlikely to help. If current research is unavailable, use supplied material and stated assumptions; do not claim a survey of current products or invent references.

## Compare visual concepts

Compare a small number of genuinely different approaches when the choice matters. Vary a meaningful decision, such as product demonstration versus a service explanation, task sequence, content density, or imagery. Use the same real content and task to compare clarity, identity, access, assets, implementation cost, and maintenance. Color-only variants are useful for a palette decision, not evidence of broader exploration.

If an open brief produces near-identical concepts, reconsider composition, density, type, color, and assets together within the allowed direction. Compare a contrasting viable approach when it could change the decision. Replacing an excluded effect with the same generic layout does not establish product fit; novelty alone is not the goal.

Use a moodboard to compare lighting, texture, subjects, or visual character. Note what to adopt and exclude. It is not a finished interface or reuse permission. Test promising ideas with real content and placements before choosing them.

Avoid anchoring on the first attractive example or assembling unrelated attractive details. Select a direction against requirements and user preferences; check that borrowed ideas still work together with actual content and constrained layouts. Involve the user when an unresolved subjective choice materially changes identity or the experience. Follow a clear brief without another approval round; keep routine reversible choices moving.

## Choose media by purpose

Choose what helps people understand, recognize, compare, act, or experience. Atmosphere is a valid purpose; neither imagery in every section nor removing all decoration is a default requirement.

| Medium | Useful role | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photography | Actual people, places, materials, products, or intended atmosphere | Context, accuracy, representation, permission, and implied factual claims |
| Illustration | Abstract relationships, concepts, or character | Suitable metaphor, clear meaning, and no misleading invented detail |
| Icons | Repeated actions, states, or categories | Recognizability, consistent meaning, actual-size legibility, and needed labels |
| Diagrams | Relationships, sequence, or mechanisms | Correct labels, direction, scope, and accessible equivalent |
| Screenshots | Actual interface, feature, or process | Current version, readable focus, real capability, and confidential content |
| Expressive imagery | Character, pacing, or visual motif | Relevant expression without obstructing work or adding disproportionate cost |
| Text or no added asset | Direct explanation or action | Whether omitting a visual loses important information or intended expression |

A conceptual image cannot prove a product capability. A tiny accurate screenshot may explain little. Keep icon meaning stable; use a familiar action pattern when a novel silhouette obscures the control. Mixed media can work when their relationship is coherent. Use [Audio & Video](audio-and-video.md) when playback, sound, or time-based interaction needs guidance.

Judge the rendered artwork rather than its file format. Do not pass rough assembled scenes, unfinished marks, or a text description off as finished product imagery or a working demo. Preserve useful SVG artwork, diagrams, intentional avatar fallbacks, and honestly scoped prototypes. If a required asset is unavailable, identify the gap instead of inventing evidence.

## Keep assets visually consistent

Keep useful recurring cues: light, viewpoint, color treatment, illustration perspective, texture, icon weight, or another property relevant to the concept. Define only what the task needs.

Compare assets together in representative contexts. Individually attractive photographs may imply different settings or activities. Preserve intentional contrast; repair accidental mismatch. Inspect icons at actual size, not just matching names.

For interface icons, use a coherent family and compatible stroke, fill, and optical weight. Prefer its designed sizes or optical variants where available; scaling a glyph need not preserve its balance beside text. [Carbon's icon guidance](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/icons/guidelines) illustrates calibrated icon/type relationships within its system. Check visible alignment as well as the bounding box. Icon artwork and the interactive target are different sizes; use [Input methods](responsive-design.md#support-different-input-methods) for the latter. Keep necessary labels and do not mirror direction-sensitive or branded artwork automatically.

Use visual storytelling when a meaningful progression helps. Do not imply a factual sequence through a montage or staged reconstruction. Repeated motifs can aid recognition without adding an illustration to every section.

Consider acquisition, updating, localization, and production capacity. A custom image for every catalog item can create unsustainable work; a commissioned focal asset may justify its cost.

## Choose assets for user needs

Each informative asset should answer a useful question. Add views when they show different information, not to fill a gallery.

For physical products, consider scale, fit, materials, construction, variants, and use context. Match dimensions and shown variants to the offered item. For software, show the relevant work or outcome with readable detail and enough context to avoid implying missing prerequisites. For services, represent people, activities, and environments accurately.

Use relevant local or subject expertise when unfamiliar representation could materially mislead. Do not infer identity, consent, or cultural meaning from appearance. Keep purposeful repetition; remove assets that add no useful information or character.

## Check cropping and placement

Inspect the actual slot and narrow/wide variants. Preserve focal subjects, meaningful detail, scale cues, and space needed by adjacent text or controls. A center crop can change meaning even when the full image is suitable.

Choose another crop, image, or art-directed variant when needed. Do not stretch a product, crop out a safety-relevant detail, or mirror text, directional diagrams, logos, or meaningful actions merely to fit the layout.

Check text contrast against the actual crop. A dark overlay may damage the image or fail locally; reposition text or choose a different asset when clearer. Use [Visual Design](visual-design.md#define-color-roles-and-contrast) for the relevant contrast decisions.

Confirm the image still contributes after masks, washes, and overlays. Follow the intended subject's contour when a cut-out is needed; do not approximate it with an unrelated shape. Keep deliberate geometric crops when they serve the composition.

Specify sufficient quality for the intended size, enlargement, and use. The largest source file is not necessarily the right delivered asset. Communicate crop, focal point, important detail, and variants; Engineering handles delivery. Keep changing text editable and localizable when possible. Integral image text still needs legibility and equivalent meaning.

## Check representation and provenance

Distinguish actual depiction, conceptual treatment, and draft placeholder. Never invent customers, facilities, testimonials, metrics, or product states and present them as evidence.

For synthetic or altered imagery, check whether viewers could reasonably interpret it as factual. Identify its illustrative status where material. Inspect text, geometry, anatomy, relationships, and product details. If accuracy cannot be established for a factual role, use a verifiable source or clearly nonliteral alternative.

Provenance, the record of an asset's origin and editing history, can help trace it. [C2PA's explainer](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/explainer/Explainer.html) distinguishes provenance from factual truth: credentials do not establish accuracy, permission, or suitability. Missing credentials do not establish deception. Do not claim validation without a suitable check or require a provenance service for ordinary work.

## Check permission for the intended use

Before distributing an asset, inspect its actual source and applicable permission. A public page, search result, download button, or citation does not establish reuse rights. An inspiration link and a redistributed asset are different uses.

Check relevant conditions: commercial use, modification, attribution, redistribution, medium, and restrictions. A project's code license may not cover its photographs or logos. For example, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) requires appropriate credit and modification notices; it does not supply every possible right or authorize implied endorsement.

Keep source, creator, terms, intended use, required credit, modifications, and unresolved restrictions in existing project notes when useful. Preserve required notices. Do not create a registry for every image or upload private material externally without authorization. Resolve a consequential permission gap or select a supported alternative before presenting the asset as cleared.

## Evaluate assets in context

Inspect actual placement, content, and relevant states. Does the asset clarify the intended information or expression? Does it preserve context and fit identity without blocking reading or action?

For example, add an accurate scale view when a furniture image does not communicate size; replace an unreadable software collage with a focused capture; retain expressive illustration when its role is clear and fix only the mismatched asset.

Use [Accessibility & Inclusive Writing](../writing/accessibility-and-inclusion.md#provide-content-alternatives) for meaningful text alternatives and [W3C's image decision tree](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/decision-tree/) for informative, functional, decorative, and complex use distinctions. The same image can need different treatment in another context.

Inspect crop integrity, legibility, source records, and content completeness directly. Claims about comprehension, trust, or cultural fit may need appropriate reader evidence. Do not report a visual inspection as measured user impact; use [Design Evaluation](design-evaluation.md) for evaluation choices.
