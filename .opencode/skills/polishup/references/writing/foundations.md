# Writing Foundations

Use for shared writing principles. Topic references can be read independently; this file is optional.

## Set writing priorities

Judge content, expression, and context together. Prioritize what readers need to understand, decide, do, or experience. Clear wording cannot replace missing information, and accurate content can still be hard to use. Brevity, formality, novelty, and familiar formats do not define quality by themselves.

## Preserve meaning and voice

When changing text, preserve actors, actions, conditions, qualifications, certainty, and distinctions between claims. Keep the author's or brand's intended character unless changing it is requested. Apply the relevant language and project conventions. Unusual expression may be deliberate; identify a problem before replacing it.

## Keep changes within scope

Distinguish a defect from a trade-off or preference. Make the change needed for the requested result and retain effective content. For review-only work, explain findings without applying edits.

Distinguish supplied facts, inferred meanings, and evidence from readers. Check what matters to the decision and risk. Keep small edits focused and report only verification actually performed.

Use the shared [Writing rules](rules.md). Topic references develop the methods for these decisions.
