# Review & Revision

Use to diagnose problems, set priorities, choose changes, evaluate feedback, or check revisions. Select relevant checks and topic references; no fixed editing sequence or full-domain read is required.

## Confirm the requested work

Identify the requested result, available material, applicable conventions, and qualities that must remain stable. Reuse the brief. Proofreading does not authorize changing the argument; a requested restructuring needs more than spelling corrections.

- **Creation:** apply relevant criteria while developing the work. A separate review report is unnecessary unless requested.
- **Refinement:** make justified changes for the agreed result, retaining satisfactory parts.
- **Review:** assess and report without changing the text. A proposed replacement can illustrate a finding; applying it requires authorization.

Limit conclusions to available material. Report serious out-of-scope concerns without silently expanding the work. Clarify conflicts that change the assignment; routine editing choices need no extra approval.

## Diagnose the problem

Connect a finding to the passage, reader need or requirement, and consequence. Read enough surrounding context to distinguish a missing argument, unclear order, language problem, tone mismatch, or preference.

| Judgment | Basis | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defect | Demonstrated error in meaning, fact, applicable convention, or explicit requirement | Correct within scope or report with evidence |
| Contextual risk or trade-off | A plausible difficulty conditional on reader or use, with a competing benefit | Explain the condition and choose a proportionate repair or seek needed evidence |
| Preference | A defensible alternative without an established advantage for this task | Preserve the original unless the brief calls for change; label optional advice |

Keep confidence separate from impact. An uncertain interpretation can matter greatly without being a confirmed defect. Re-read, verify, or ask when alternative meanings require different edits. A polished or specific comment is not necessarily an important finding.

## Choose the editing approach

| Work | Use when | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Developmental or structural editing | Purpose, coverage, argument, narrative, or arrangement needs development | Resolve substantive gaps before polishing dependent text; never invent facts |
| Stylistic or line editing | Connections, expression, or cadence need attention within a useful draft | Preserve intended character and justified unusual expression |
| Copy editing | Mechanics, terminology, references, or consistency need correction | Use the actual language and project conventions; do not imply exhaustive fact-checking |
| Proofreading | Prepared content needs correctness, completeness, or presentation checks | Inspect the supplied or delivered version; avoid discretionary rewriting |
| Rewriting | The authorized transformation cannot be achieved by local repair | Establish the meaning, commitments, evidence, and voice that must survive |

Editing terms and levels vary and can overlap. Agree on the actual work instead of assuming a label includes every activity. Prefer local corrections when sufficient; move a section when that solves more than many sentence edits. Do not rewrite a working document to fix one name.

## Prioritize by impact and dependency

Address errors that most affect the intended outcome: wrong facts, changed meaning, missing conditions, unsupported conclusions, unanswered questions, or unusable instructions. A negation or decimal can matter more than several awkward paragraphs.

Resolve problems before editing text that depends on them when this avoids rework. Sentence-level defects can themselves change an argument; prioritization is not a rule that small edits must become developmental reviews.

With limited time or access, inspect consequential uncertainty first and identify the coverage limit. A sample of checked claims is not verification of the whole document. Group related findings when they have a shared cause. No fixed pass count, severity formula, or minimum number of findings is required.

## Evaluate feedback

Separate a reviewer's reported difficulty from their proposed solution. Accept, adapt, decline, or query the suggestion according to its evidence and effect on the text. A request for a different introduction may not solve an unsupported conclusion.

For conflicting comments, compare purpose, assumptions, authority, and consequences. Repeated difficulty may reveal a communication problem. Check whether reviewers encountered it independently or repeated the same source. Repetition and majority preference do not prove a factual claim. Clarify conflicting requirements with the responsible user when needed. Preserve the author's judgment. Use existing comments when useful; not every edit needs a response letter or change log.

## Revise and check affected content

Keep the original for comparison when needed. Edit the relevant passage and check its context, definitions, references, examples, captions, headings, and repeated facts. A local correction can create repetition or contradictions elsewhere.

Use [Content & Evidence](content-and-evidence.md#preserve-meaning-when-summarizing-or-revising) for claims, conditions, and attribution, [Voice & Tone](voice-and-tone.md) for the author's character, and [Multilingual Writing](multilingual-writing.md) for preserving meaning across languages. Preserve exact quotations, identifiers, names, and required wording according to their role.

Preservation does not require keeping a verified error or rejecting an authorized new direction. Correct established errors within scope and make consequential claim changes visible. If the intended meaning is unknown, ask or flag it rather than selecting a convenient interpretation.

| Question | Suitable check | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did the repair solve the problem? | Compare the revised passage and context with the original criterion | A visible change or smoother wording does not prove improvement |
| Did meaning or support change? | Compare important statements and their conditions, inspect the source, or recalculate a changed value | A diff identifies changes but does not establish accuracy or preservation of meaning |
| Are mechanics and repeated choices correct? | Check the applicable authority, terminology, or style record; use suitable search/checker tools | Checkers can miss context or flag valid names, dialect, and creative effects |
| Is affected content complete and connected? | Inspect links, references, captions, headings, and repeated facts | A working URL does not establish source support |
| Can readers use the delivered version? | Inspect the actual medium; seek appropriate reader or subject/language feedback when consequential | Text-only review does not establish comprehension, cultural fit, or product accessibility |

A spelling repair may need only the word and affected context checked. A substantial rewrite needs comparison for both intended improvement and regressions. Use the available method; no particular diff tool, checker, model, or harness is required.

## Check final output and report limits

Use self-review for a specific question and check important claims against their sources. Compare a revision with the original; fluent wording can still change meaning. Another prompt, session, model, or score does not alone establish independence or correctness. Do not launch additional model sessions or benchmarks without authorization.

When final presentation matters, inspect the version readers will receive if available. Confirm requested corrections and their consequences. At proofreading stage, avoid optional rewriting that creates new checking or layout work without clear benefit. Report serious defects rather than polishing around them.

Before delivering finished work, remove unintended chat introductions, editing notes, and unusable citation markers. Resolve missing required facts or identify what prevents completion; never fill gaps with invented content. Preserve descriptive placeholders in requested drafts or templates, intended code variables, and protected source text. Mark an incomplete draft clearly rather than presenting it as finished copy. Match formatting to the destination, including plain text where Markdown will not render.

Report location, issue, consequence, and proposed action or question. Separate defects, uncertain concerns, and preferences. Do not invent criticism; state when no material issue was found within the inspected scope.

For applied refinement, deliver the artifact and explain significant decisions or unresolved limits when useful. Stop when the requested outcome is met, relevant checks support it, and further changes have no clear benefit. If evidence or a requirement remains missing, identify it instead of claiming the document is fully verified, error-free, or accessible.
