# Multilingual Writing

Use for translation, localization, transcreation, source-meaning checks, or language-specific delivery requirements. Reuse established audience and style decisions. Use [Accessibility & Inclusive Writing](accessibility-and-inclusion.md) when access barriers or representation need separate attention.

## Choose the adaptation approach

Identify source and target languages, relevant variety or locale, purpose, and permitted changes when they affect the result. Reuse the existing brief and terminology. A short task does not require a separate localization plan.

| Approach | Primary obligation | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Translation | Convey source meaning in appropriate target-language expression | Syntax and idiom may change; unsupported additions, omissions, and commitments are not authorized |
| Localization | Adapt content and relevant conventions for a locale | A language is not one country. Formatting a value does not authorize changing it or inventing a local fact |
| Transcreation | Rework expression for an agreed communicative or persuasive effect | Establish creative freedom. A rewritten slogan may no longer preserve each statement individually |

Terminology can vary. Clarify important conflicts between preserving source meaning and creative adaptation rather than silently choosing one.

Preserve the intended relationship, register, and effect using suitable target-language forms. A joke, honorific, metaphor, or address can need a different expression. Use relevant language and cultural evidence instead of national stereotypes. Flag an unresolved consequential nuance or seek qualified review.

## Check meaning against the source

Keep a reliable source version. Compare consequential passages for actors, actions, recipients, negation, quantities, time, conditions, certainty, attribution, and defined terms. Check omissions, additions, untranslated text, and technical terms used with the wrong meaning. Fluent target wording can still carry the wrong message.

For example, translating "Administrators may export selected records" as "All users can export every record" changes permission, actor scope, and content scope. Restore those distinctions even if the target sentence sounds fluent.

Preserve names, diacritics, identifiers, placeholders, and quotations according to their role. Distinguish transliteration, an official translated name, and a new translation when relevant. If the source is ambiguous or appears wrong, ask or flag the issue instead of silently correcting it while claiming source fidelity. Use [Content & Evidence](content-and-evidence.md#preserve-meaning-when-summarizing-or-revising) for questions about preserving source meaning.

## Review target-language quality

Check whether wording, register, terminology, grammar, and conventions fit the target audience and intended use. [Bilingual revision and monolingual review](https://translation.ec.europa.eu/languages-and-translation-european-commission/translation-quality_en) check source correspondence and target suitability respectively. Neither replaces the other; adapt the method without requiring an institutional workflow.

Preserve specialist depth where required. Subject expertise, language proficiency, literacy, and cultural familiarity are distinct. Avoid flattening an author's character or replacing local expression with a generic assistant voice. Use [Voice & Tone](voice-and-tone.md) for expressive decisions.

Prioritize important or uncertain passages, considering the language pair, subject, and source quality. Use qualified bilingual or subject review when errors could affect decisions or actions. Check actual capability for less-supported language pairs instead of assuming performance transfers from another pair.

Another generated version, translation back into the source language, model agreement, or a favorable score can reveal problems but does not prove correctness or independent verification. State what was checked and the affected uncertainty when adequate review is unavailable. Do not claim a verified or publication-ready translation without the necessary evidence.

## Preserve values and delivery requirements

Resolve ambiguous numeric dates from verified context. Do not guess whether a date uses day/month or month/day. Preserve quantities when adapting decimal separators, units, currency, or time presentation. An actual conversion or change to locale-specific facts needs a reliable basis.

For reusable messages, provide context for the speaker, action, and placeholder meanings. Keep placeholder identities and meaningful boundaries intact. Flag plural forms, agreement, word order, text expansion, or sentence fragments that require delivery changes; translated fragments are not automatically interchangeable.

Identify relevant language changes and text-direction needs. Preserve required characters and diacritics. Word translation alone does not verify mixed-direction rendering, assistive-technology pronunciation, or language-switch behavior. Not every borrowed word requires different language markup; applicable exceptions matter.

Writing supplies meaning, language identification, alternatives, and adaptation constraints. UI/UX checks presentation and interaction; Engineering checks encoding, semantics, direction, plural handling, associations, and rendered behavior. Report these implementation needs without claiming they have been built or tested.

## Verify the requested result

Check the target against both the source and the intended use. Preserve the authorized degree of adaptation, and keep facts, conditions, values, names, and placeholders intact unless a change was expressly required and supported.

For review-only work, identify the affected source/target passage, the discrepancy or suitability problem, its consequence, and a proposed correction or question. A request to translate does not automatically require an accessibility audit or complete product-localization assessment.
