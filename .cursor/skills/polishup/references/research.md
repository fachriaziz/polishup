# Task Research

Use for unfamiliar fields, uncertain quality criteria, conflicting evidence, or material gaps in task research. The root skill requires initial research; this reference explains how to make it useful. It does not supply specialist expertise or require a separate report, fixed source count, or new tools.

## Establish context

Inspect the brief and relevant existing work. Identify the deliverable, audience or users, intended use, requested action, constraints, and what must remain unchanged. Check the medium, destination, software version, and available capabilities when they affect the method or result.

Separate stated requirements, observed conditions, assumptions, and unresolved choices. Ask when a consequential gap has no defensible default; otherwise state material assumptions and continue. Public research cannot establish a private preference or authorize a redesign. For a source-only transformation, research the method without adding outside facts to the transformed content.

## Research methods and failure modes

Start from the task and the decisions it requires. Look for applicable professional methods, quality criteria, standards, and relevant examples. Investigate concrete failures alongside successful practice, including documented AI-output problems. Do not frame every query to confirm that a pattern is bad. If the field lacks reliable AI-slop research, use established failure criteria without inventing a blacklist or claiming prevalence.

Choose sources for their role:

- Official documentation establishes supported behavior, requirements, and version-specific methods.
- Original research or a sound synthesis supports findings within its methods, population, and conditions.
- Professional guidance and first-hand case studies offer methods and trade-offs; one successful project does not establish a universal optimum.
- Reviews and community reports can reveal recurring concerns. Check their examples and context before generalizing.
- Galleries and reference work show possibilities and visual choices, not proof of usability, correctness, or effectiveness.

Open the relevant content rather than relying on search snippets or generated summaries. Check authorship, evidence, dates, applicable versions, and important limitations. Use current sources for changing practice; retain older fundamentals when still applicable. A recently updated page may describe an experimental version rather than the target environment. Compare consequential disagreements and trace repeated claims to their original evidence. For deeper source assessment, use [Content & Evidence](writing/content-and-evidence.md#evaluate-sources).

Use suitable connected tools or official documentation through native search/browser tools. Keep private material and credentials out of external queries. Do not install software, contact participants, publish experiments, or upload artifacts merely to satisfy a research step without authorization.

## Turn findings into decisions

Translate material findings into the method to use, why it fits, constraints or exceptions, and what to check. Distinguish a requirement from a heuristic or aesthetic preference. Retain source links and version context where they support consequential decisions or useful follow-up, without attaching a bibliography to every minor edit.

Compare meaningfully different approaches when the brief leaves an important choice open. Do not generate variants for a settled direction or imitate an entire reference. Preserve existing identity and conventions unless changing them is within scope. Modern methods can support traditional, expressive, restrained, or experimental work.

Examples of task-specific decisions:

| Task | Research and check |
| --- | --- |
| A 3D asset | Intended use and destination determine topology, materials, lighting, and delivery checks. A still render does not establish suitability for deformation or real-time use |
| A data analysis | Establish data definitions, missingness, units, comparison, and assumptions before choosing a method. Verify calculations and interpretation; polished charts do not establish a valid conclusion |
| Learning material | Match explanations, examples, and practice to learner knowledge and objectives. Check correctness and alignment; readability alone does not establish learning outcomes |
| A narrow UI repair | Verify relevant guidance, then check the affected component and states. Research does not justify replacing the visual identity or building a design system |

These examples identify research questions, not complete domain instructions. For high-stakes work, use applicable specialist standards and review; do not imply professional assurance that the evidence cannot support.

## Check the actual result

Use checks appropriate to the artifact, scope, and available tools: inspect rendered output, exercise behavior, calculate results, compare with source material, or review against explicit criteria. Preserve meaning, required behavior, and satisfactory work. Address concrete defects before cosmetic preferences.

Keep claims separate: a source was read, a method was chosen, an artifact was produced, and its behavior was tested are different observations. Report consequential gaps. A screenshot cannot establish interaction behavior; an attractive render cannot establish the quality of hidden geometry; source verification cannot establish user outcomes.

Follow-up research should answer a remaining question rather than repeat the initial search. Stop when the requested result has adequate support and proportionate checks. If access fails, retry transient failures within reasonable bounds or use another suitable source. If evidence remains unavailable, narrow, qualify, defer, or ask about the affected decision while completing independent supported work. Never invent research, measurements, currentness, or validation.
